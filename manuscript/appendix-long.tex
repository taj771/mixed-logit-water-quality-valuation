\subsection*{A11. Choice Set Randomization}
\addcontentsline{toc}{subsection}{\protect\numberline{A3}Choice Set Randomization}

\textbf{Study Area}

Within the study area, there are four river basins across Saskatchewan, Alberta, and Manitoba and 18 sub-river basins.

\noindent\textbf{Versions}


There are four survey versions that respondents are randomized into at the beginning of the survey. These versions differ 
on the current water quality (WQ) conditions presented to respondents:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Version 1
    \item Version 2
    \item Version 3
    \item Version 4
\end{itemize}    


\noindent\textbf{Treatments}

There are two overall survey treatments that respondents are randomized into at the beginning of the survey:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Spatial
    \item WQ
\end{enumerate}


Thus, at the beginning of the survey, respondents will have been randomized into a Version--Treatment pair.

\noindent\textbf{Blocks}

Within each of these versions/treatments, respondents will receive two blocks of choice scenarios. These blocks contain between 2 
and 7 choice sets each, all grouped under a unique block number. There are a total of 114 blocks.

\noindent\textbf{Local vs. Non-local Blocks}

The two blocks of choice scenarios offered to each respondent include:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Local block: river basin includes the respondent’s home location.
    \item Non-local block: sub-river basin does not include the respondent’s home location.
\end{enumerate}


The local definition depends on the home location of the respondent based on their postal code (survey question S6). While the local 
blocks are endogenous to the respondent’s home location, the non-local units are randomly assigned from non-local river basins.

\noindent\textbf{Spatial Treatment}

Within the spatial treatment, there are two types of blocks that depend on the spatial scale:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item River basin
    \item River sub-basin
\end{enumerate}


Within each spatial unit block, respondents are presented with choice sets that hold the spatial scale fixed with various WQ change scenarios. 

For example, if a respondent’s postal code is in the South Saskatchewan river basin and they are randomly assigned Version~1 and the spatial 
treatment, we will randomly allocate one of the five South Saskatchewan spatial blocks (i.e., local block) and one of the 17 non-South 
Saskatchewan spatial blocks (i.e., non-local block) (see Table A3.1).

\noindent\textbf{WQ Treatment}

Within the WQ treatment, there are four types of blocks that depend on the changes in WQ level:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Improve WQ to level 1
    \item Improve WQ to level 2
    \item Improve WQ to level 3
    \item Improve WQ level by 1 unit
\end{enumerate}

Within each WQ change block, respondents are presented with choice scenarios with varying spatial units but holding the level of WQ 
improvement constant.

For example, if a respondent’s postal code is in the North Saskatchewan river basin and is randomly assigned Version~2 and the 
WQ treatment of the survey, we will randomly allocate one of the four WQ change blocks as their local block and one of the ten non-North 
Saskatchewan WQ change blocks as their non-local block (see Table A3.1).


For each block there will be on average 2--7 voting proposals, depending on the river basin, as some choices were excluded because they
were redundant or unnecessary.
